# Known sender labels in Outlook: staff guide

{{COMPANY}} now uses a small add-on to Outlook that tells you whether an email
comes from a sender we have personally checked and approved. This guide explains
what you will see and what to do. It takes about five minutes to read.

## What you will see

Incoming emails get one of two labels near the top of the message (and in the
message list):

- **✓ Known sender** (blue): the email came from an exact email address or business domain that one of our registrars has independently verified, and Microsoft's mail system confirmed it was not forged.
- **Unknown sender** (gray): we have not verified this sender. This is normal for new customers, suppliers and anyone else we have not checked yet. It does not mean the email is bad.

Labels usually appear within about 15 minutes of an email arriving. If an email
has no label yet, treat it as **Unknown sender**.

The labels appear in Outlook on the web, Outlook on your computer and the Outlook
phone app, including shared mailboxes. The checkmark is part of the label text;
it is not a Microsoft security badge.

## What the labels do NOT mean

> A Known sender is not a guarantee that an email is safe.

- A real supplier's mailbox can be hacked and used to send fake invoices.
- A Known sender can still send a harmful link or attachment by mistake.
- Microsoft's normal spam, phishing and malware protection still runs on every email. The label adds business context; it does not replace that protection.

## The golden rules for money and account changes

1. **Never change bank details, payment instructions or remit-to addresses based on an email alone**, even from a Known sender.
2. **Call to confirm** using a phone number you already had on file (from a contract, an old invoice or our records), never a number in the email.
3. **Be extra careful with Unknown sender emails that look like an existing supplier.** Criminals register lookalike domains that differ by a single letter (for example {{LOOKALIKE_EXAMPLE}}). Read the actual email address, not just the display name.
4. **Report anything suspicious** using Outlook's **Report** button (Report phishing) and tell {{SUPPORT_CONTACT}}.

## Common questions

**A customer I know shows Unknown sender. Is something wrong?**
No. They simply have not been verified yet. Ask a registrar ({{REGISTRARS}}) to verify them.

**Should I remove or change the labels?**
Please don't. They are managed automatically. If you remove one, it may not come back until that sender's status changes.

**I don't see the colors.**
The label text is what matters. Some phone and desktop views show less color.

**Who can approve a sender?**
Only the registrars: {{REGISTRARS}}.

# Registrar guide

This section is for {{REGISTRARS}}: the people allowed to approve senders.

## Before you approve anyone

Approving a sender tells everyone in the company "this is a real business
contact." Only approve after you have **independently** confirmed ownership,
for example:

- A callback to a phone number you already had on file (not one from the email).
- Confirmation in person with an established contact.
- An existing contract or customer record that you verified independently.
- For a whole company domain: independent confirmation that the company owns it.

An email signature, a previous conversation, or the email itself is **not**
verification.

## Option A: the Verify sender button in Outlook

1. Open the email from the sender you want to approve.
2. Select **Verify sender** (in the message toolbar or the Apps menu; in the phone app, tap the three dots, then the Sender Registry icon).
3. Check the email address shown. The display name is not proof of identity.
4. Choose **Verify this exact address** or **Verify the whole business domain**. Sender Registry opens with the sender filled in.
5. Complete the form (see "Filling in the form" below) and select **Verify sender**.

The button only fills in the form. Nothing is approved until you complete it.

## Option B: Sender Registry directly

1. Open Sender Registry: {{REGISTRY_LINK}}
2. Select **Verify a sender**.
3. Fill in the form and select **Verify sender**.

## Filling in the form

- **What are you verifying?** Choose *This email address only* to approve one exact address (use this for Gmail, Outlook.com, Yahoo and other personal providers, or when you only know one person). Choose *This exact business domain* to approve every address at that company domain; subdomains are not included and shared providers such as Gmail cannot be approved this way.
- **Business or customer name**: who they are, for example "Supplier Inc."
- **How did you independently verify ownership?** Pick the method you used.
- **Restricted evidence reference**: a short note of where your proof is kept, such as "Callback 10/6 to number in vendor file, by Mike". Do not paste email content, passwords or documents.
- **Review or expiry date**: defaults to one year. The approval stops working after this date until renewed.
- Tick the confirmation box and select **Verify sender**.

Within about 15 minutes, that sender's emails from the last 30 days change to
**✓ Known sender**, and new emails arrive labelled Known.

## Renewing and revoking

- **Renew**: in **Verified senders**, select **Renew** next to the entry and re-confirm. Do this before the expiry date.
- **Revoke**: select **Revoke**, give a reason, and confirm. Do this immediately if a supplier reports a hacked mailbox, changes ownership, or you are unsure. Their emails return to **Unknown sender** within about 15 minutes.

When in doubt, revoke. You can always verify again later.
